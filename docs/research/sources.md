# Sources

Sources for the 0.2.0 wording and implementation extension are recorded separately in the [proposal review](improvement-review.md), including exact sections and applicability limits.

Register for the [research report](foundations.md). Access checked on 2026-09-09. Dates below are publication/revision dates, not search-engine crawl dates. University mirrors contain original works, not university endorsements. Full text was accessible unless stated otherwise. No papers are redistributed here.

1. **Pamela Zave and Michael Jackson (1997).** [Four Dark Corners of Requirements Engineering](https://cse.msu.edu/~chengb/RE-491/Papers/dark-corners-re-zave-jackson.pdf). *ACM TOSEM* 6(1), 1–30. DOI: 10.1145/237432.237434. Conceptual/formal analysis; sections 2–5 support grounded terminology and requirements/specification/domain distinctions. Not an LLM evaluation.

2. **Axel van Lamsweerde (2001).** [Goal-Oriented Requirements Engineering: A Guided Tour](https://webperso.info.ucl.ac.be/~avl/files/RE01.pdf). *RE 2001*, 249–263. DOI: 10.1109/ISRE.2001.948567. Author-hosted research synthesis: refinement, responsibility, conflicts, obstacles. Selected ideas do not constitute KAOS implementation or inherit formal guarantees.

3. **Bashar Nuseibeh (2001).** [Weaving Together Requirements and Architectures](https://oro.open.ac.uk/2213/). *Computer* 34(3), 115–117. DOI: 10.1109/2.910904. Position article. Repository metadata/abstract and an indexed author-paper excerpt were accessible; direct full-text retrieval failed. Used only for its central concurrent-refinement proposal.

4. **David L. Parnas (1972).** [On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules](https://www.cs.lafayette.edu/~gexia/cs301/resources/parnas.html). *CACM* 15(12), 1053–1058. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1145/361598.361623). Comparative design argument using a worked example; information hiding, not a universal modularity optimum.

5. **Philippe Kruchten (1995).** [Architectural Blueprints—The “4+1” View Model of Software Architecture](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~gregor/teaching/papers/4%2B1view-architecture.pdf). *IEEE Software* 12(6), 42–50. Architecture-description method/experience; concurrent views linked by scenarios. Selected views here do not imply method conformance.

6. **ISO/IEC/IEEE (2022).** [42010:2022: Software, systems and enterprise — Architecture description](https://www.iso.org/standard/74393.html). Official public overview only; normative text not accessed. Supports description vocabulary scope, not clause-level interpretation or a conformance claim.

7. **Felix Bachmann, Len Bass, and Mark Klein (2003).** [Deriving Architectural Tactics: A Step Toward Methodical Architectural Design](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/documents/704/2003_005_001_14213.pdf). CMU/SEI-2003-TR-004. DOI: 10.1184/R1/6573047.v1. Technical report; section 2 and Appendix A connect concrete quality scenarios and tactics through worked examples.

8. **Orlena C. Z. Gotel and Anthony C. W. Finkelstein (1994).** [An Analysis of the Requirements Traceability Problem](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/749/1/2.2_rtprob.pdf). *First International Conference on Requirements Engineering*, 94–101. Empirical investigation; pre-/post-specification traceability. Historical practitioner population and tool context limit generalization.

9. **Thomas R. Gruber (1993).** [A Translation Approach to Portable Ontology Specifications](https://tomgruber.org/writing/ontolingua-kaj-1993.pdf). *Knowledge Acquisition* 5(2), 199–220. DOI: 10.1006/knac.1993.1008. Revised KSL report; explicit conceptualizations and relationships for interoperability. Not evidence of prompting performance.

10. **Eric Evans (2015).** [Domain-Driven Design Reference: Definitions and Pattern Summaries](https://www.domainlanguage.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/DDD_Reference_2015-03.pdf). Author-published practitioner reference, March 2015. Bounded Context and Ubiquitous Language sections; authoritative method definitions, not an academic outcome study.

11. **Clayton Lewis and John Rieman (1993/1994).** [Task-Centered User Interface Design: Chapter 1](https://cspages.ucalgary.ca/~tam/2001/hci_topics/papers/LewisRiemanBook/chap-1.html). Academic authors' practical text, university mirror. Representative tasks and prototype evaluation; walkthroughs do not replace user evidence.

12. **Alistair Mavin, Philip Wilkinson, Adrian Harwood, and Mark Novak (2009).** [Easy Approach to Requirements Syntax (EARS)](https://ccy05327.github.io/SDD/08-PDF/Easy%20Approach%20to%20Requirements%20Syntax%20%28EARS%29.pdf). *RE 2009*, 317–322. DOI: 10.1109/RE.2009.9. Original paper, public mirror; structured language patterns and industrial experience. Syntax is not semantic validation.

13. **Martín Abadi and Leslie Lamport (1993).** [Composing Specifications](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Composing-Specifications.pdf). *ACM TOPLAS* 15(1), 73–132. Formal composition/refinement work. Temporal and environment assumptions matter; prose contracts are not its proof method.

14. **Jerome H. Saltzer, David P. Reed, and David D. Clark (1984).** [End-to-End Arguments in System Design](https://web.mit.edu/Saltzer/www/publications/endtoend/endtoend.pdf). *ACM TOCS* 2(4), 277–288. Architectural argument and examples; sufficient knowledge for function placement. Does not mandate placing every responsibility in applications.

15. **Jerome H. Saltzer and Michael D. Schroeder (1975).** [The Protection of Information in Computer Systems: Basic Principles](https://web.mit.edu/Saltzer/www/publications/protection/Basic.html). *Proceedings of the IEEE* 63(9), 1278–1308. Foundational protection principles. Relevant to authority boundaries; not an audit of this skill or MochiOS.

16. **Melvin E. Conway (1968).** [How Do Committees Invent?](https://www.melconway.com/Home/pdf/committees.pdf). *Datamation*, April, 28–31. Organizational design argument about communication constraints; not a causal measurement of agent-team effectiveness.

17. **Alan MacCormack, Carliss Baldwin, and John Rusnak (2012).** [Exploring the Duality between Product and Organizational Architectures: A Test of the “Mirroring” Hypothesis](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Research%20Policy%2041%20%282012%29%201309%E2%80%93%201324_c5c2350e-013c-4065-a2f9-d95eb32177d5.pdf). *Research Policy* 41, 1309–1324. Comparative empirical study of software systems. Association does not make current team boundaries architecturally optimal.

18. **Barry W. Boehm (1988).** [A Spiral Model of Software Development and Enhancement](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~markk/SWE_copies/boehmspiral.pdf). *Computer* 21(5), 61–72. Risk-driven process proposal with experience; supports proportional investigation rather than fixed research quotas.

19. **Butler W. Lampson (1983; revised reprint).** [Hints for Computer System Design](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/acrobat-17.pdf). Original *SOSP/Operating Systems Review* 17(5), 33–48; revised in *IEEE Software* 1(1), 1984. Contextual experience, not universal recipes. Some host metadata has inconsistent volume details; original venue information used here.

20. **Ralf Jung, Jacques-Henri Jourdan, Robbert Krebbers, and Derek Dreyer (2018).** [RustBelt: Securing the Foundations of the Rust Programming Language](https://plv.mpi-sws.org/rustbelt/popl18/paper.pdf). *POPL / PACMPL* 2, Article 66. Machine-checked soundness for a modeled subset and verified unsafe extensions. Does not prove arbitrary Rust applications correct.

21. **Arshia Hemmat, Mohammadreza Sharbaf, Shekoufeh Kolahdouz-Rahimi, Kevin Lano, and Sobhan Y. Tehrani (2025).** [Research Directions for Using LLM in Software Requirement Engineering: A Systematic Review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1519437/full). *Frontiers in Computer Science* 7:1519437, March 21. Original review study; landscape and limitations, not a substitute for underlying experiments.

22. **Tobias Eisenreich, Husein Jusic, and Stefan Wagner (2026).** [Automating Domain-Driven Design: Experience with a Prompting Framework](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26244v1). arXiv:2603.26244v1, March 27. Preprint; one company, three models, detailed German requirements. Sections V–VI report useful early artifacts and accumulating later errors. No publication-status inference from its IEEE copyright placeholder.

23. **Haoxiang Jia, Robbie Morris, He Ye, Federica Sarro, and Sergey Mechtaev (2025).** [Automated Repair of Ambiguous Problem Descriptions for LLM-Based Code Generation](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07270v3). arXiv:2505.07270v3, September 24 revision. Benchmark experiment/repair method. Current title differs from the initially indexed title. Function-level results do not establish architecture effectiveness.

24. **Zheng Fang, Dongming Jin, Yihong Dong, Yongmin Li, Kechi Zhang, Zhi Jin, and Ge Li (2026).** [ClarifyCodeBench: Evaluating LLMs on Clarifying Ambiguous Requirements for Code Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00711v2). August 31 revision. Preprint abstract/metadata inspected; the abstract retains an unresolved “N” ambiguity-type count. Exploratory lead only; no numerical or primary design claim relies on it.

25. **Wayland project (undated).** [Wayland Architecture](https://wayland.freedesktop.org/architecture.html). Official engineering documentation. Input-to-render path and client/compositor responsibilities; some implementation details are historical and need fresh verification before backend selection.

26. **Fuchsia project (updated 2025-02-28).** [Flatland](https://fuchsia.dev/fuchsia-src/concepts/ui/scenic/flatland). Official documentation. Independent scene submissions and system composition. Architectural precedent, not evidence of portability to a different kernel or graphics environment.

Register for [modular-redesign.md](modular-redesign.md). Access checked on 2026-11-12. "Full text", "abstract", "landing page", or "not accessed" states what was actually read; claims in the report stay within that. Sources marked not accessed are cited only for a well-known method definition.

27. **Anthropic (accessed 2026-11-12).** [Skill authoring best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices). Official vendor documentation, full text. 28. **Anthropic (2025-10-16).** [Equipping agents for the real world with Agent Skills](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills). Engineering blog, full text. Three-level progressive disclosure; design claims, not measurements.

29. **Jeremy Hadfield, Barry Zhang, Kenneth Lien, Florian Scholz, Jeremy Fox, and Daniel Ford (2025-06-13).** [How we built our multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system). Engineering blog, full text. Internal evaluation: 90.2% over single-agent Opus 4; token usage explains 80% of variance (with tool calls and model choice, 95% on BrowseComp); about 4× tokens for agents and 15× for multi-agent versus chat; dependency-heavy domains a poor fit. Vendor-internal benchmark, not independently replicated.

30. **Gloaguen et al. (2026).** [Evaluating AGENTS.md: Are Repository-Level Context Files Helpful for Coding Agents?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11988) arXiv:2602.11988v3. Abstract only; author name from the search index, not confirmed on the page. Context files do not generally improve success and raise cost over 20%; instructions are followed; overviews unhelpful. Task counts not stated in the abstract.

31. **Jai Lal Lulla, Seyedmoein Mohsenimofidi, Matthias Galster, Jie M. Zhang, Sebastian Baltes, and Christoph Treude (2026).** [On the Impact of AGENTS.md Files on the Efficiency of AI Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20404). arXiv:2601.20404v2, March 30. Abstract only. 10 repositories, 124 pull requests; median runtime Δ 28.64%, output tokens Δ 16.58%, comparable completion. Output tokens only.

32. **Authors not recorded (2026).** [Do Context Files Help Coding Agents? A Two-Agent Ablation Study on Real Repositories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27250). arXiv:2607.27250. Abstract only. Claude Code and Codex, 17 tasks from 3 repositories, 288 runs; correctness effect bounded to at most 10–15pp; failures in implementation skill. Small task set.

33. **Authors not recorded (2026).** [Instruction Adherence in Coding Agent Configuration Files: A Factorial Study of Four File-Structure Variables](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10039). arXiv:2605.10039. Abstract only. 1,650 Claude Code sessions, two TypeScript codebases; no detectable structural effect; compliance odds about 5.6% lower per additional generated function (post hoc).

34. **Daniel Jaroslawicz, Brendan Whiting, Parth Shah, and Karime Maamari (2025).** [How Many Instructions Can LLMs Follow at Once?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538) arXiv:2507.11538v1. Abstract only. IFScale; 20 models; best reaches 68% accuracy at 500 instructions. Synthetic keyword-inclusion task, not coding.

35. **Authors not recorded (2026).** [A Systematic Survey of Agentic Skills: Architecture, Lifecycle, and Security](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29596). arXiv:2608.29596v2. Abstract only. Nine-stage skill lifecycle; survey, no experiments.

36. **Mert Cemri, Melissa Z. Pan, Shuyi Yang, et al. (2025).** [Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657) arXiv:2503.13657v3, revised October 26. Abstract only. 14 failure modes in 3 categories; taxonomy from 150 traces (κ = 0.88); 1,600+ traces over 7 frameworks. Intervention figures from earlier versions not used.

37. **Hangfan Zhang, Zhiyao Cui, Jianhao Chen, et al. (2025).** [Stop Overvaluing Multi-Agent Debate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08788). arXiv:2502.08788v3. 38. **Yongjin Yang, Euiin Yi, Jongwoo Ko, Kimin Lee, Zhijing Jin, and Se-Young Yun (2025).** [Revisiting Multi-Agent Debate as Test-Time Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22960). arXiv:2505.22960v2, preprint under review. 39. **Saaket Agashe, Yue Fan, Anthony Reyna, and Xin Eric Wang (2025).** [LLM-Coordination: Evaluating and Analyzing Multi-agent Coordination Abilities in Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.448/). *Findings of NAACL 2025*, 8053–8072. ## Sources for optimization (2026-11-12)

Register for [optimization.md](optimization.md). Access checked on 2026-11-12. Sources [27]–[29] and [34] above are also used.

52. **Huiqiang Jiang, Qianhui Wu, Chin-Yew Lin, Yuqing Yang, and Lili Qiu (2023).** [LLMLingua: Compressing Prompts for Accelerated Inference of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05736). *EMNLP 2023*. Abstract only. "Up to 20x compression with little performance loss" on GSM8K, BBH, ShareGPT, and Arxiv-March23. Not tested on agent instructions.

53. **An Empirical Study on Prompt Compression for Large Language Models (2025).** [arXiv:2505.00019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00019). Search summary only; authors not recorded. LLMLingua variants lead at high ratios; long-context performance degrades past an optimum ratio. Used only to support the degradation caveat.

54. **Tobias Lindenbauer, Igor Slinko, Ludwig Felder, Egor Bogomolov, and Yaroslav Zharov (2025).** [The Complexity Trap: Simple Observation Masking Is as Efficient as LLM Summarization for Agent Context Management](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433). arXiv:2508.21433v3; DL4C workshop at NeurIPS 2025. Abstract only, plus a vendor blog for tuning notes. SWE-agent on SWE-bench Verified, five model configurations: masking "halves cost relative to the raw agent" while matching or slightly exceeding summarization's solve rate; hybrid saves a further 7% and 11%.

55. **Anthropic (accessed 2026-11-12).** [Prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching). Official documentation; first ~40,000 characters read verbatim, the rest only summarized. Cache reads 0.1× base input (0.05× on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5; 0.025× on Fable/Mythos 5.1 and 5.2); writes 1.25× (5-minute) and 2× (1-hour); prefix order tools, system, messages; minimum cacheable length 512 tokens on Haiku 5.5. Prices change; recheck before projecting costs.

56. **Lingjiao Chen, Matei Zaharia, and James Zou (2023).** [FrugalGPT: How to Use Large Language Models While Reducing Cost and Improving Performance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176). arXiv:2305.05176v1. Abstract only. Cascade matches GPT-4 "with up to 98% cost reduction" or improves accuracy 4% at equal cost. Benchmark-specific upper bound; 2023 models.

57. **Isaac Ong, Amjad Almahairi, Vincent Wu, Wei-Lin Chiang, Tianhao Wu, Joseph E. Gonzalez, M Waleed Kadous, and Ion Stoica (2024).** [RouteLLM: Learning to Route LLMs with Preference Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665). arXiv:2406.18665v4. Abstract only. Costs reduced "by over 2 times in certain cases" without compromising response quality; chat benchmarks, not agentic work.

58. **Lakshya A Agrawal, Shangyin Tan, Dilara Soylu, et al. (2025).** [GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457). *ICLR 2026* (oral). Abstract only. Beats GRPO by 6% on average (up to 20%) "using up to 35x fewer rollouts"; beats MIPROv2 by over 10%. No prompt-length claim in the abstract; length behavior comes from unverified practitioner reports.

59. **Donald J. Schuirmann (1987).** A Comparison of the Two One-Sided Tests Procedure and the Power Approach for Assessing the Equivalence of Average Bioavailability. *Journal of Pharmacokinetics and Biopharmaceutics* 15(6), 657–680.
